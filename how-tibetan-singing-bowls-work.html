The sound of the bowl

Why does a singing bowl sing?

A Tibetan singing bowl is a metal bowl that can produce a sustained, layered tone when struck or played around its rim. Rather than producing just one simple note, the bowl can create a combination of tones that blend together into its characteristic resonant sound.

The sound depends on factors such as the bowl's construction, shape, thickness and the way it is played. Different bowls can therefore have noticeably different tonal qualities.

For meditation, the important part is often not analysing every frequency. It is the experience of giving your attention to a clear, sustained sound.

A single sustained sound can give the mind a simple point of attention — something to listen to rather than something to think about.

The mechanics

Three things create the sound.

The distinctive voice of a singing bowl comes from the interaction between its physical construction and the way it is played.

01

Vibration

When the bowl is struck or rubbed around its rim, the metal vibrates. Those vibrations move the surrounding air and become audible sound.

02

Resonance

The bowl's shape and construction allow vibrations to continue for a period of time, creating the sustained resonance that makes singing bowls so recognisable.

03

Harmonics

Multiple related frequencies can occur together. Their combination contributes to the complex, evolving character of the sound.

Sound as an anchor

Listening can become the meditation.

Meditation does not always require silence. For some people, a gentle and predictable sound provides a useful anchor for attention.

Instead of following a guided voice, you can simply notice the sound as it begins, develops, fades and eventually disappears.

This creates a very simple practice: hear the sound, notice when attention wanders, and gently return to listening.

Focus Use sound as a simple object of attention.

Meditation practice

How to use singing-bowl sound in meditation.

You do not need a complicated routine. The same principle can be applied using a physical bowl, recorded bowl sounds or other calm ambient audio.

Begin with a clear intention

Choose how long you want to sit and decide that, for the duration of the session, the sound will be your main point of attention.

Listen without analysing

Notice pitch, resonance, volume and the way the sound changes. There is no need to decide whether the sound is good or bad.

Return when the mind wanders

Thoughts will naturally appear. When you notice that attention has moved elsewhere, gently return to the sound.

Finish deliberately

When the timer ends, take a moment before moving immediately into the next activity. Let the end of the sound mark the end of the practice.

Make sound part of your routine
