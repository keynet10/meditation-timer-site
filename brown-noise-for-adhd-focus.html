Brown noise is a continuous broadband sound with more emphasis on lower frequencies than white noise. For some people, that deeper sound provides a consistent background that makes an otherwise distracting environment feel more settled.

What is brown noise?

Brown noise is a type of noise signal containing a broad range of frequencies, with relatively greater energy in the lower frequencies. It is often perceived as deeper and softer than white noise.

Why use it for focus?

A steady background sound can mask some intermittent environmental sounds and provide a consistent auditory backdrop while you work. Individual responses vary, so the most useful approach is to experiment with volume, duration and the type of sound that suits you.

Brown noise and attention

Brown Noise and ADHD: A Practical Focus Tool

People with ADHD can experience difficulty maintaining attention in distracting environments. Brown noise is one environmental strategy some people choose to experiment with during work or study. It should be viewed as a practical listening tool rather than a treatment for ADHD.

Reduce competing sound

Conversations, traffic, household activity and sudden noises can interrupt a concentration session. A continuous background sound may make some of those changes less noticeable.

Create a repeatable environment

Using the same sound and timer structure can help turn a particular setup into a familiar focus ritual. Start the sound, set the session and begin the task.

Keep the volume comfortable

Louder is not necessarily better. Use a comfortable listening level that remains in the background rather than becoming another source of distraction.

Try different sounds

Brown noise is not universally preferred. Some people prefer white noise, pink noise, nature sounds, ambient music or silence. The goal is to find the environment that lets you concentrate comfortably.

Choose your listening environment

Brown Noise vs White Noise vs Nature Sounds

Different background sounds create different listening experiences. Experimenting with several options can help you find what works for your own focus routine.

Brown Noise

Deep, low-frequency-heavy broadband noise. Often chosen when a softer, deeper background sound is preferred.

White Noise

A brighter broadband noise containing a broad spread of frequencies. Some listeners prefer it for masking environmental sounds.

Nature Sounds

Rain, water, wind and other ambient recordings can provide a more organic alternative to continuous noise.
