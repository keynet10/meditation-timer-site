Understanding white noise

A constant sound
for a quieter environment.

White noise is a broadband sound containing energy across a wide range of audible frequencies. It is often perceived as a steady, hiss-like sound and can be used as background audio when people want to reduce the contrast between silence and intermittent environmental sounds.

What is white noise?

White noise is a continuous sound signal containing a broad range of frequencies. Because it is consistent, some people use it as an auditory background for work, sleep or relaxation.

Why combine it with a timer?

A timer gives the listening session a defined beginning and end. Instead of watching the clock, you can allow the sound and timer to provide the structure while you focus on meditation, rest or work.

One sound, several uses

White noise can fit
into different routines.

White noise is not limited to meditation. Depending on your preferences, it can become part of a sleep routine, focus session, relaxation practice or background listening environment.

Meditation

Some people prefer a subtle background sound rather than complete silence during unguided meditation. A steady sound can provide something neutral to notice while the timer keeps the session structured.

Sleep

A continuous background sound can become part of a consistent bedtime environment. Keeping the volume comfortable and using a timed session can make it easier to include sound without leaving it running indefinitely.

Focus & Study

White noise can provide a consistent auditory backdrop while studying or working, particularly when intermittent environmental sounds are distracting.

For a deeper sound, explore our Brown Noise for ADHD Focus resource.

Relaxation

A simple continuous sound can also be used as background audio during quiet reading, breathing exercises, stretching or other low-stimulation routines.

Find your preferred sound

White noise is one
of several possibilities.

There is no single background sound that suits everyone. The right choice depends on the environment, the activity and your personal listening preference.

White Noise

A broad, consistent sound that is often perceived as bright or hiss-like. Useful when a continuous auditory backdrop is preferred.

Brown Noise

A deeper, lower-frequency-heavy sound. Some listeners prefer its softer and more rumbling character for focus, study or relaxation.

Nature Sounds

Rain, water, wind and other ambient sounds provide a more organic alternative to continuous noise.
